# AD errata

The ADLXX Greek is the online Apostoliki Diakonia text, kept exactly as AD prints it, slips included. Where the text has a slip, the gloss and grammar follow the intended word, and the slip is recorded here. A slip here is an error in the publisher's online text; the printed edition has not been checked and may read correctly. A comparison script checks each AD verse word by word against three witnesses:

- **Swete**, *The Old Testament in Greek* (1887–1894), which prints Codex Vaticanus, and Alexandrinus where Vaticanus is lost. The digitization is First1KGreek (Open Greek and Latin, University of Leipzig, CC BY-SA 4.0). It is read locally only. Nothing of its text is reproduced here; the list says only whether Swete agrees.
- **Athens**, the second edition in this project.
- **Brenton's Greek** (1851, public domain, eBible `grcbrent`), the text behind Brenton's English and Boyd's revision.

Words are compared without accents, breathings or case, with movable ν and ς folded and ει/ι and ρρ/ρ treated as spellings of one sound. An AD word that none of the witnesses has in its verse is a **probable slip** when a word one letter away (two for words of seven letters or more) stands in at least two witnesses. Swete lacks Ecclesiastes, so there one witness is enough. Words of three letters or fewer are never counted as misprints, since there a one-letter difference is a different word (ὅτι, καί). Where the alignment with Swete replaces the AD word one for one and two witnesses agree, the word is a **reading**: AD's text differs, not its printing. Accent-only differences are not seen.

## Where the slips arose

Every electronic copy of AD's Old Testament we have found goes back to one typing: the publisher's website, Myriobiblos (Genesis 1 and Job 1 match word for word), the eBible `grclxx` text, and the Greek in the "Palea Diathiki" EPUBs on the Internet Archive. All of them print κώμην for κόμην at Job 1:20. A second copy therefore cannot show whether a slip is in the printed book or only in the electronic text; only the print can. Michael Asser, who translated from the 1997 printed edition, remarked in the introduction to his 2014 edition that the online text departs from the print in a few small points; he did not list them. Until the print is checked, this list calls them slips in the AD electronic text.

In nearly every book, AD stands closer to Brenton's Greek than to Swete. Brenton's text descends from the Sixtine edition of 1587 by way of Valpy. Part of Swete's lower figure is OCR noise in the digitization, but the gap holds book after book. The exceptions are 1 Esdras, Tobit, Daniel and 4 Maccabees, where Brenton prints a different text form; Sirach and Ezekiel are about even. For checking a reading in AD, Brenton–Boyd is therefore the closer English; the Lexham English Septuagint, which follows Swete, is the more distant one. Swete still helps where Brenton and AD part.

## The list

[adlxx-errata.tsv](adlxx-errata.tsv) holds 1,841 places where AD's word differs from the witnesses, each with a verdict:

| Verdict | Count | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| slip | 560 | AD's word is not a real form, or makes no sense in its verse, and the witnesses' word is what AD means (λέγων for λέων at 1 Kings 13:24; σὺ for οὐ at Deuteronomy 7:25) |
| reading | 949 | a real word that reads correctly; AD's text differs (θυγατέρες for θυγατέρας, Lamentations 4:3; many name forms) |
| spelling | 332 | the same word spelled another way (ἐξολοθρ-/ἐξολεθρ-, ἔνατος/ἔννατος, εἰσέλθετε/εἰσέλθατε) |

Two slips were found later, in the review of AD's lemmas: Γάβερκαὶ at Deuteronomy 2:8, where Γάβερ and καί are printed as one word, and συναντὴν at 2 Kings 2:15, for συνάντησιν.

Four reviewer agents read the 1,942 candidates from the letter comparison in their verses, with the AD, Athens and Brenton verses side by side; Swete's agreement was shown only as yes or no. 139 turned out to be misalignments, where AD does have the witnesses' word, and are left out; two received no verdict. A reading needed only to make sense; a slip claim needed AD's form to be impossible or senseless. Of the 558 slips, 522 come from the letter comparison, 34 from the grammar review (breathings, accents and iota subscripts, which the comparison cannot see: ὅρη for ὄρη, ᾦ for ὦ, ἦς for ᾖς), and two were noted by hand and missed by the comparison (κενόν for καινόν at Ecclesiastes 1:10; στρεφομένους at Isaiah 33:18, which Athens numbers 19).

For each slip the list gives the intended word, accented as Athens or Brenton prints it. AD's surface stays as printed; the gloss and grammar follow the intended word. Where they had followed the slip, they were corrected: 95 glosses (᾿Ασσοὺδ "Assud" is now "Asshur"; Κεδουρὰν "Kedorah" is now "Hadoram"), 343 grammar strings and 314 lemmas. The lemmas were set from the lemma the intended word carries elsewhere in AD, or in Athens where AD lacks it, and replaced placeholder forms such as ἐπιστήμιω (for ἐπιστρέφω) and ἀκώθην (for κάθημαι). AD also writes the aorist of αἴρω and its compounds with iota subscript (ᾖρε, ἐξῇρε, ἐπῇρεν), about 200 times; that is a spelling, and the grammar keeps the aorist. Fifteen words found only in Swete are not listed, so that none of its text is reproduced.
